Master's thesis · Apr 2026 – present

Causal impact of Lucky Loser entry on tennis careers

Does one lucky break change a career? I built a longitudinal dataset of 3,000+ player-month observations across 140+ tennis players and 16 years, integrating ATP rankings and match results with Python and LLM-assisted pipelines. I test Grand Slam Lucky Loser selection against random-draw expectations, then use a quasi-experimental Difference-in-Differences design to estimate the causal effect on long-term career outcomes.

  • 3,000+ player-month observations
  • 140+ players · 16 years
  • DiD causal design

Python · causal inference · Difference-in-Differences · hypothesis testing

Same method answers: did a policy, treatment, campaign or process change really move the outcome?

Try it · how a lucky loser is chosen

One withdrawal. Four players. One lucky break.

When a player withdraws from a Grand Slam main draw, the four highest-ranked players who lost in the final round of qualifying go into a random draw. The one picked gets the spot (treated). The other three stay out (control). Their rankings and situations are nearly identical, so luck is the only difference.

    Now follow both groups over time. What changed because of the lucky break?

    Illustrative toy simulation based on the design in my thesis proposal, not results. One draw is noisy, so the real study repeats this across many Grand Slam draws and uses Difference-in-Differences with player and time fixed effects.
